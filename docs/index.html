THE ATHLETE

The person behind NorthLine 2.0

Damiano Zanoni

ENDURANCE · CURIOSITY · LEARNING

My name is Damiano Zanoni. I have always been drawn to long distances, mountains and challenges that force me to discover what I am capable of. For me, endurance is not simply about resisting pain or collecting kilometres. It is about staying clear-minded when the body is tired, accepting uncertainty and continuing to learn while moving forward.

The first NorthLine did not end the way I had imagined. I started in Chiasso with the goal of crossing Switzerland from south to north, but my attempt failed before I reached Bargen. It was difficult to accept, yet it taught me more than a successful crossing probably could have. I learned that determination is essential, but it cannot replace a sustainable strategy.

With NorthLine 2.0, I am not trying to erase that failure or pretend it never happened. I am returning to the challenge with a different approach: better control of my pace, more respect for recovery and greater attention to the decisions that can determine the entire journey. This time, I want to reach Chiasso not simply by pushing harder, but by moving smarter.

ONE COUNTRY. ONE DIRECTION.

The second NorthLine. One athlete, no support car.

The journey begins in Bargen and ends in Chiasso: around 300 kilometres from north to south, undertaken alone and without vehicle support.

WHY NorthLine 2.0?

The horizon is always ahead.

We never reach it by staring at it. We reach it by moving toward it. NorthLine 2.0 is the idea of continuing forward as a team, even when the destination is still far away. One kilometre becomes the next. One step becomes another. Until the horizon becomes the finish line.

KEEP MOVING SOUTH.

THE CHALLENGE

What is NorthLine 2.0?

NorthLine 2.0 is not a repeat of the first crossing. Starting in Bargen, Damiano heads south towards Chiasso without a support vehicle or a ready-made solution when plans change.

Food, equipment, navigation, charging and recovery must all function as one moving system. Independence is not just a slogan here; it shapes every kilometre.

Weather, fatigue and unexpected setbacks will continuously reshape the plan. Every decision—from when to rest to where to find water or recharge—must be made along the way, using only what can be carried or found on the route.

The live platform turns the crossing into a shared experience. Follow the current position, completed track, statistics and field notes as the journey unfolds.

The goal is not simply to reach Chiasso. It is to prove that the entire crossing can work as one self-sufficient journey from north to south.
